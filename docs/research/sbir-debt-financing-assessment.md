# Debt financing and security interests in the SBIR program

**Prepared for:** SBA and participating-agency analysts considering how private
capital should inform SBIR/STTR performance measurement

**Audience:** program analysts, economists, and counsel

**Evidence status:** Exploratory and non-citable. This note assesses public-data
capabilities and measurement choices. It does not determine the financing,
eligibility, or benchmark status of any concern.

## Finding

Public records can show that an SBIR/STTR concern has used secured credit, but
they usually cannot show the cash received, current balance, use of proceeds, or
relationship to an SBIR/STTR project. The records are useful for source discovery,
prepopulation, and risk-based verification. They are not sufficient by themselves
to calculate a private-investment credit.

An exploratory pilot found more public evidence of secured-credit relationships
than of issuer equity financing. That difference may reflect financing behavior,
disclosure rules, and the lack of a stated common event period for the two
signal types. It should not be interpreted as a program-wide capital-structure
estimate.

## Terminology

This note uses **debt financing** for borrowed money and **security interest** for
a creditor's interest in specified collateral. A bank loan, equipment financing,
or Uniform Commercial Code financing statement is not necessarily a debt
security under federal securities law. Form D debt and convertible offerings are
securities offerings. The distinction matters because the public reporting
systems cover different transactions.

## What public sources can establish

| Source | Useful public fields | What the source does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| SEC Form D and D/A | issuer, first-sale date, security type, offering amount, cumulative amount sold, exemption | complete private financing, cash retained by the operating concern, current balance, collateral, or use of proceeds |
| State UCC registries | debtor, secured party, filing date, jurisdiction, sometimes collateral, amendments, continuations, and terminations | amount advanced, amount drawn, current balance, purpose, or whether a filing supports a new advance rather than a refinancing |
| USPTO assignment records | recorded security agreements, mortgages, liens, releases, affected patents, and parties | loan amount, patent value, current debt balance, or whether intellectual property was the primary underwriting basis |
| SEC periodic reports and exhibits | facility commitments, draws, maturity, pricing, covenants, guarantors, and collateral for reporting companies | comparable coverage for private concerns |
| Bank, lender, and investment-fund disclosures | selected facility amounts, lender role, security level, and sometimes pricing or maturity | complete market coverage, draw amounts, and reliable allocation among operating subsidiaries |

The SEC describes Form D as a notice filed after the first sale in a Regulation D
offering. It is not a census of private financing. The USPTO likewise records
documents affecting patent ownership, including security interests, mortgages,
and liens, but recordation does not measure the underlying loan.

**Non-public source.** The SBIR Company Commercialization Report holds actual
additional-investment dollars and project attribution. The concern supplies them
through SBIR.gov, and SBA does not publish them
([commercialization benchmark method](../commercialization-benchmark-methodology.md)).
A public-data analysis cannot obtain them. They are not independently verified
unless SBA or an agency requests support.

## Exploratory aggregate pilot

The pilot used a 30-concern proxy population defined by more than 25 deduplicated
Phase II awards in fiscal years 2020 through 2024. It reviewed public equity,
ownership, secured-credit, and filing evidence through November 19, 2026. No
company-level result is reported in this note.

The award years select the 30 concerns. They do not set the period in which
financing events were counted. November 19, 2026 is the review cutoff for both
signal categories. This note does not record a financing-event start date for
either category. Secured-credit evidence can also predate the award window,
because a UCC filing stays on record until it is terminated. The equity and
secured-credit counts below therefore do not cover a stated common period. Do not
compare them as rates.

| Aggregate observation | Count |
|---|---:|
| Concerns with affirmative firm-level secured-credit evidence | 15 of 30 |
| Concerns with a publicly stated secured-facility amount | 3 of 30 |
| Concerns with an equity, convertible, ESOP, or acquisition signal | 9 of 30 |
| Concerns with confirmed issuer cash proceeds from a reviewed equity offering | 1 of 30 |
| Concerns with at least one reviewed capital-structure signal | 18 of 30 |
| Concerns with neither signal established | 12 of 30 |
| Concerns for which public-source coverage was materially limited | 9 of 30 |

These figures are source-coverage diagnostics, not financing-incidence estimates.
An unobserved filing is not evidence that a concern had no debt or outside capital.
State access differs, private-company disclosure is sparse, and entity-name
collisions can create false Form D matches. In the pilot, 11 of 13 automated
Form D name candidates were different legal entities.

## Interpreting secured-credit activity

Secured credit can support several different activities:

- working capital while the concern waits for contract payment;
- equipment, facilities, or manufacturing scale-up;
- project development and commercialization;
- acquisitions or ownership changes;
- employee stock ownership plan transactions;
- shareholder distributions or recapitalizations; and
- refinancing of an existing obligation.

Those uses do not carry the same policy meaning. Project-development and
production financing may show that an outside lender underwrote a technology or
commercial plan. Working-capital credit may instead reflect payment timing and a
federal-contract backlog. Acquisition, ESOP, and distribution debt principally
finance ownership. Refinancing can create a new filing without providing new
capital.

A patent security interest also has limited meaning by itself. Lenders commonly
include intellectual property in a blanket collateral package. The filing shows
that a creditor claimed an interest in the patents. It does not show that the
lender valued the patents separately, that the debt financed commercialization,
or that the borrower was distressed.

## Measurement choices

### 1. Use public debt data only as context

SBA could retain the existing Company Commercialization Report treatment of
additional investment and use UCC, USPTO, and lender records to prepopulate
possible financing events or select records for review. This has the lowest
classification burden and avoids assigning value to liens.

### 2. Create a separate project-linked debt measure

SBA could credit actual net new principal received for an identified SBIR/STTR
technology during the measurement window. This would align with the statutory
database concept of additional investment used to further award research. It
would require the concern to report draws, purpose, project attribution, and
refinancing amounts.

### 3. Recognize a broader external-financing measure

SBA could measure external cash received by the concern from equity and debt,
while reporting project-linked and general corporate financing separately. This
would answer a broader capital-access question. It would not, without additional
evidence, measure Phase III success or commercialization of a Phase I project.

## Minimum rules if debt receives credit

Count only actual cash principal received by the assessed concern during the
measurement period. Do not use the face amount of a UCC filing or an undrawn
facility commitment.

Report these categories separately:

1. project-linked research, development, production, or commercialization credit;
2. equipment and facility financing;
3. general working-capital credit;
4. acquisition, ESOP, or other ownership-transaction financing;
5. related-party financing; and
6. refinancing.

Exclude or separately disclose:

- principal used only to refinance existing debt;
- acquisition consideration or distributions paid to owners;
- undrawn commitments;
- ordinary trade credit and operating leases;
- federal title or reversion interests in government-funded equipment;
- intercompany journal entries without traceable external cash; and
- a conversion or amendment that does not provide additional cash.

The same dollar must not be counted as revenue, equity investment, and debt
investment. A facility supporting several projects must be apportioned once.

## Verification and burden

The existing Company Commercialization Report already instructs firms to report
actual investment dollars received, exclude Phase I and II funds, avoid double
counting, and apportion investment across contributing projects. A debt schedule
could build on that structure rather than rely on Form D or UCC records as the
legal measure.

A minimal schedule would request:

- closing and funding dates;
- principal actually advanced during the period;
- lender and related-party status;
- instrument and collateral category;
- use of proceeds;
- refinancing and repayment amounts; and
- associated SBIR/STTR projects and allocation.

Public records could prepopulate lender, date, and collateral fields. Full loan
documents should be requested only when a claimed amount changes the outcome,
involves a related party, conflicts with public evidence, or enters an audit
sample.

## Limits

- UCC law and public portal access vary by jurisdiction.
- The filing jurisdiction may follow legal organization rather than operating
  address.
- A filing can remain of record after repayment, and a termination can lag.
- Continuations and amendments are lifecycle events, not new financing.
- Form D amendments are cumulative and can be double-counted if summed.
- Patent recordation is voluntary in important respects and is not a valuation.
- Public-company disclosures are more complete than private-company disclosures.
- Public evidence cannot establish a program-wide rate without a declared
  jurisdictional sampling frame and a complete identity review.
