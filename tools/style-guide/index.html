Theme

Interactive style guide · prototype

Form carries how much you can trust it.

The distinguishing move in this repository is that visual form encodes epistemic standing, not brand. A dashed edge is not a stylistic variant of a solid one — it means the relationship is a name candidate rather than an identifier-verified observation. That idea is real, it is good, and it is currently implemented four separate times in four unrelated palettes.

This guide applies the repository's own discipline to its design system: every convention below is labeled by how much weight it can carry.

The missing keystone

Two synced skills — sbir-lit-refresh and sbir-spec-status-survey — instruct agents to call apply_figure_style() from a figure-style skill. That skill does not exist on disk. The status-survey skill acknowledges this and tells the agent to apply the conventions by hand. A shared figure style is already assumed by the tooling and simply was never written; the most useful thing this prototype could turn into is its specification.

The lineage strip

Horizontal position is not layout — it is the funding lineage stage, fixed per record kind in kindDefinitions[].column. Money flows left to right: agency, to NSF award, through a technology area, to a legal entity, to DoD award.

Edge thickness is logarithmic in dollars, clamped to a 0.8–5 px band: max(0.8, min(5, 0.8 + log10(amount + 1) / 3)). The clamp is the point — a $2B prime contract must not visually erase a $150k Phase I award. Opacity separately encodes persistence: 0.34 + min(0.36, fiscal_years × 0.07).

Encoding status

Three channels carry meaning independently: shape says what kind of record it is, hue says which funding world it belongs to, stroke says whether the claim is verified. Because they are independent, the graph survives greyscale and colour-vision deficiency.

Squares are institutions, rounded rectangles are money instruments, the diamond is a classification, circles are legal persons. Tier is encoded by column position and shape, never by a dedicated hue — Tier 1 primes reuse the DoD teal and Tier 2 awardees reuse the entity amber.

Stroke · evidentiary standing CONVENTIONAL

Verified
Candidate

Solid edges are direct or identifier-verified observations. Dashed edges — dash array [7, 5], always in the candidate hue — are name candidates, CET text classifications, or temporal associations. Driven by isCandidate(), which keys off edge.candidate, an evidence_grade containing "candidate", or match_confidence === "candidate_name".

Interface tokens

In the explorer stylesheet each token is declared once, as light-dark(light, dark) under color-scheme: light dark. This page restates the same tokens across three blocks — :root for the light values, @media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) for the OS-dark values, and :root[data-theme="dark"] so the rail's toggle can override the OS. Only values are redefined in the dark blocks; no component is styled inside them. Both values are listed whatever theme you are in, because the pair is the unit of design. Select a row to copy its var() reference.

TokenLightDarkMeans

The canvas never hardcodes these. It resolves them at paint time by reading each custom property with getComputedStyle(document.documentElement).getPropertyValue() (the cssVar helper below) — so the graph re-themes with the OS and the stylesheet stays the single source of truth. It is the best pattern in the repository and nothing else uses it.

On weight 650

Not a rounding of 600 or 700. It appears seven times in the shipped stylesheet, always on interface labels that must out-rank body text without reading as a heading. Inter carries it as a variable axis; the fallback stack degrades to 700, which is acceptable — the hierarchy survives, only the finesse is lost. No webfont is loaded, by design: the explorer is dependency-free and must render offline from a local http.server. Matplotlib figures set no font family at all and render in DejaVu Sans, so charts and interface do not currently share a typeface.

Components

Two of these are specific to this project and carry most of its character. Both exist to make an interpretive limit impossible to scroll past.

Guardrail

Interpretation boundary: Observed DoD funding is not proof that a specific NSF-funded capability was used.

Page level, below the graph, not dismissible. Muted text on a neutral --edge rule: it is a boundary, not a warning. Styling it as an alert would both overstate it and invite dismissal.

Screening block

Match confidence is name-based only. No identifier link is present in the source tables for this pair.

Node level, tinted --supplier-soft with a solid --entity rule. Rendered only when a specific record has an evidentiary caveat, so its presence is itself information.

Prose conventions

Markdown is the largest rendered surface in this repository, and its conventions are the only ones with a CI script behind them. Bold run-in labels replace fourth-level headings throughout, because MD036 is disabled precisely to allow it.

Tier declaration ENFORCED

**Target epistemic tier:** `evidence`

Line 3 of a spec, exactly one per file, matched by a regex in scripts/ci/check_epistemic_tiers.py against a frozen set of four values. Missing, duplicate, and invalid declarations fail CI. This is the only piece of the visual language a machine will stop you from breaking.

Status banner CONVENTIONAL

> **Status (2026-08-14):** Phase 0 design only. No implementation is
> authorized. The criteria are **not frozen**. Every artifact this spec
> produces is **non-citable** (`citable: false`) until the gates pass.

Blockquote with a bold lead-in. No GitHub [!NOTE] admonitions appear anywhere in the repository. A lighter variant puts **Status:** as a plain bold line directly under the H1; both forms are live, which is the one wrinkle here. Nested bold is the intensifier: **Status:** **FROZEN as Revision 1**.

Normative in specs/tech-area-transition-report/publication-format.md and enforced downstream in generated reports, where an absent channel renders as Not computed — not zero. This is the prose equivalent of the dashed edge: the absence of evidence gets its own visible form rather than defaulting to a zero.

Emoji policy CONVENTIONAL

Unwritten and unevenly observed. 169 emoji occurrences sit in 17 markdown files. Archive directories hold most of them, but 9 live files still carry 68 — docs/architecture/duckdb-cet-analysis.md alone has 42, with the remainder spread across specs/company-categorization/tasks.md, specs/phase3-match-benchmark/, docs/research/dod-form-d-leverage.md, and several docs/development/ and docs/architecture/ standards pages (exception-handling.md, logging-standards.md, asset-naming-standards.md). So the convention is real in new prose and has never been enforced retroactively.

Two corrections worth keeping visible, because this page argues that unverified transcription is the failure mode. Nothing enforces this: .markdownlint.yaml has no emoji rule, so its archive exclusions are not why archives keep theirs. And the three-value quality scale — ✅ at ≥0.95, ⚠️ at ≥0.85, ❌ below — is not the only emoji code emits: 18 live Python files print emoji, including ℹ️ in scripts/performance/detect_performance_regression.py and 📊 and 🔍 elsewhere.

The tier ladder

The same trust grammar applied to artifacts rather than marks. A tier fixes what an artifact costs to maintain and how much weight it may carry. Select a tier for its contract.

Two rules keep the ladder honest. Nothing is promoted by becoming useful, gaining importers, or having its numbers quoted — promotion is explicit work against the destination contract. And evidence admits no partial credit: all four requirements, or the artifact is pipelines.

Known conflicts

Places where the codebase contradicts itself. Each is a decision waiting to be made, not a bug to be quietly patched — picking a winner changes published figures.

--technology and --critical are the same value

Both resolve to #047857 light and #6ee7b7 dark. A CET-area diamond and a critical-supply-chain review ring are indistinguishable by hue; only shape and stroke separate them. Defensible, since the channels are independent — but it should be a stated decision rather than a coincidence.

The per-agency panel has two colors

Orange #FF9800 in the M&A-signal and Form-D-temporal figures, teal #00897B in prime-acquisitions. Same panel role, same right-hand position, different hue. A reader comparing the three figures side by side will assume the difference means something.

Two hues are double-booked

#2196F3 and #FF9800 mean "keyword cohort" and "CET-proxy cohort" in the cohort builder, but simply "left panel" and "right panel" in three other figures. A reader who learns the cohort meaning will misread the rest.

Red and green mean opposite things in adjacent files

html_templates.py renders a positive delta in red #e74c3c and a negative one in green #27ae60; html_processor.py uses green for .status-good. Meanwhile the spec-status-survey skill explicitly instructs new figures to avoid red/green opposition entirely. Three positions, one repository.

Two unrelated blues for "defense"

Material #1565C0 in matplotlib, teal-blue #155e75 as --dod-award in the explorer. Same concept, no shared token. The four greens are worse: #4CAF50 patent cohort, #047857 CET area, #2d6a2d high confidence in mermaid, #27ae60 status-good.

That agreement is the natural seam along which to unify them.
