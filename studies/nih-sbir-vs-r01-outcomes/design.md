# NIH SBIR Versus R01 Outcomes Design

## Question

Compare NIH SBIR and R01 research performance on common outcomes and lifecycle costs.

## Estimand

Estimate a vector of adjusted outcome differences between comparable NIH portfolios.
Do not collapse the vector into one program ranking.
Do not interpret the adjusted difference as causal without an exogenous assignment rule.

## Cohorts

Reproduce the National Academies 2010–2015 award cohorts first.
Define research topic, institute, start year, lifecycle obligations, project duration, and research stage.
Keep firms and academic performers visible as different populations.

## Outcomes

Measure operating-firm survival, private capital, licensing, FDA approvals, clinical use, and non-SBIR revenue.
Measure value added or productivity when access permits.
Retain publications and patents as intermediate knowledge outputs.

## Comparison

Normalize each outcome by lifecycle federal cost and exposure time.
Use exact stratification before model adjustment.
Report overlap, unmatched strata, missingness, and sensitivity to matching rules.

## Failure conditions

Do not claim that an SBIR-R01 difference identifies performer efficiency.
Performer type, research stage, award size, and selection remain joint confounds.
Do not count FDA approval, licensing revenue, and downstream profit twice.
